Speaker notes

Lead with the deposit rate. Oct 1–12 is compared with Sep 1–12, the same elapsed days, not with all of September: a partial month against a full one is not like for like. The dashed line is the pooled average of the complete months only (Apr–Sep), a ratio of sums rather than a mean of monthly rates, and it is computed, so it moves as months enter and leave the window. Oct 1–12 sits above it. Each funnel row was tested with a two-proportion z-test and a 95% interval on the difference. Pending is shown rather than dropped; it is 2.5% of Oct 1–12. A control chart was tested and not used: with 3-sigma p-chart limits around the pooled rate, 1 of 6 complete months fall outside (Jul). With so few months flagged the chart adds little over the average line, and it answers a different question (is the process stable?) from the one the room asks. The plain average line answers whether this window is better or worse than usual.

Speaker notes

The distribution is the exhibit, not the median: a median can hold still while the shape moves, and only the shape shows whether decisions are piling up later. Each panel shows the share of reviewed checks decided on day 0 to day 7 after receipt, with the median day highlighted, and the badge shows the median in the first and latest month. Shapes were compared between Sep and Oct 1–12 with a chi-square test: Instant p = 0.62; Standard Review p = 0.07; Enhanced Review p < 0.01. Queue-months under 100 checks would be flagged and not read as a change; none fall under it here.

Speaker notes

Start from the members with a loss-driven reissue in Oct 1–14, then count every loss-driven card each has had in the trailing 12 months (Oct 15, 2025 to Oct 14, 2026). The window is 12 months on purpose: in a 14-day window almost everyone shows one card, so the concentration is invisible. Over a year, 9.2% of the cohort are repeats and they hold 19% of its loss-driven cards: a short, specific list for fraud review rather than a card-production problem.
